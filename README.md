# lane-centering

Feature branch of [Spysypilot](https://github.com/SpysyWeeb/Spysypilot) — see the [`combo`](https://github.com/SpysyWeeb/Spysypilot/tree/combo) branch for the full fork overview. This fork is entirely vibe-coded, is a personal project, and is **not meant for others to use** — anyone is welcome to try it at their own risk.

**Status: in progress; awaiting owner field validation.** Driven on a long highway road trip towing a trailer (owner: "did great"), with one complaint: in construction zones where traffic ran half on the shoulder, it pulled the car back toward the cones, even after a nudge on the wheel. The fade and the driver push below are the answer and have not been driven yet. Watch for a slow weave with a period of 8–10 s on a straight highway, for the car running wider than before through left curves, for how much room it still gives a cyclist, a parked car or a line of cones, whether a push away from the cones now stays, and on roads with only one painted line for whether it stays centred or pulls toward the missing line. Calm steering, a filter on the torque controller's friction term on highway straights for the "small corrections that jump too quickly" reported with centering on and off, was driven on 2026-11-04 (route 000001c3): the fast grain fell as replayed, but the owner still felt the wheel switch left and right every half second, and the logs place that oscillation in the model's plan itself (0.5–2 Hz, the same size in curves, followed by the car 0.33 s later), not in the torque controller; it is tracked separately. The speed edge now opens the gate fully at the owner's cruising speed and is not driven.

## What it does

Keeps the car in the middle of the lane lines the model sees. The driving model is content to sit off-center: on the 2021 Hyundai Palisade it rides 0.21 m left of the midpoint between the lines on average (0.14 m with the previous model), is more than 0.56 m off for a tenth of the time, and cuts curves by about 0.4 m to the inside. Left to itself it drifts back toward *its own* preferred spot, not the center, and slowly: it closes about 6 % of an offset per second.

This branch adds a small steering correction toward the lane center on top of the model's. The model still drives: the correction is at most 0.2 m/s² of lateral acceleration (the model's own demand is 1.1 m/s² at the 90th percentile), and it lets go when the driver or the model means to leave the lane. With one line painted over or worn away it keeps going from the other line and the lane width it learned while both were visible.

## Live comparison toggle

**Settings → Dev → Lane Centering** switches the correction on or off without restarting or disengaging. It defaults **on** to preserve this branch's existing behavior, and the persistent `LaneCenteringEnabled` setting survives restarts.

- **On:** adds the existing lane-centering correction, ramping in from zero through its existing filter.
- **Off:** removes the added correction and resets its filter. The model's desired steering still passes through the normal curvature limits, so the delivered command can take time to settle after a switch.
- A background worker reads the setting about every 0.1 s, outside the real-time steering loop. Direct control-process startup/replay also uses the registered default when the key is absent. The toggle does not bypass lateral-activity gates or the lateral maneuver tool.
- This changes actual steering, **not the displayed model path**; the model output itself is untouched. Have a passenger operate the toggle while driving, or change it while stopped.

The live toggle has local control-loop and UI-callback regression tests; on-device timing and road A/B behavior still need owner validation. Disengage if the comparison produces unexpected steering or weaving.

## How it works

Every model frame, `get_lane_center` looks one second ahead (at least 10 m) and compares the midpoint of the two lane lines with where the model's plan puts the car, over the plan points up to that distance. The road's curvature is in both, so only the offset between them is left. A straight line fitted to that offset gives where the plan sits and which way it is heading relative to the lane; the target error is the offset plus twice the heading's contribution at the lookahead, bounded to ±0.5 m, so a plan heading away from the center is corrected before the gap has opened and a plan already coming back is left alone. The correction is the curvature of the arc that closes 20 % of that error by the lookahead, `0.2 · 2 · error / lookahead²`, added to `modelV2.action.desiredCurvature` in `controlsd` before `clip_curvature`, so stock's lateral jerk and acceleration limits still apply to the sum. A 0.25 s first-order filter at 100 Hz smooths the 20 Hz steps.

**One line missing.** While both lines are visible the lane width at the lookahead is learned through a 5 s filter; a width that differs by more than 0.4 m for five consecutive frames replaces it outright, because that is a new road, not noise. When exactly one line is valid, the center is that line plus or minus half the learned width, for as long as the line stays valid and the width is less than 60 s old. The learned width cannot be checked against the line that is gone, only against the model's plan, which sees the whole road: if the center it gives moves more than 0.25 m relative to the plan compared with the last frame that had both lines, the width is wrong for this road and the correction stops.

It fades out instead of fighting:

- **No lane:** a line below 0.5 probability or above 0.3 m standard deviation does not count; with both lines, a lane narrower than 2.5 m or wider than 4.5 m (merges, splits, misread lines) gives no correction; with one line and no learned width, none either.
- **Leaving the lane on purpose:** steering pressed, either blinker, or a lane change in progress — the correction decays to zero over the filter's time constant.
- **The model means it:** full correction up to 0.5 m between the plan and the center, tapering to none at 0.9 m, measured on the same error the correction closes: where the plan is heading, not only where it is at the lookahead. Corner cutting sits below 0.5 m and is corrected; a plan 0.9 m off center has the wheels over the line, which is a decision (an obstacle, a lane change without a blinker), not drift. A plan that has started to move over, like the first second of a lane shift around cones, is let go as soon as it heads there, instead of being fought at full strength until it arrives.
- **The driver agrees with the model:** a push on the wheel, with the driver's torque the way the model was leaning, that moves the plan at least 0.1 m further that way and leaves it at least 0.25 m off center, tells the correction that the model means it. The torque is what tells a push from a hand resting on the wheel: the correction is off while the wheel is pressed, so the model moves the car its way too. The correction then stands down and the car drives exactly like stock openpilot, until the first of: the model's plan comes back within 0.15 m of the center, two minutes pass, either blinker or a lane change, lateral control going inactive, or a push back toward the center. Presses less than half a second apart count as one push, and the push is judged half a second after the last release; the correction comes back at the release as before, so a touch costs no more centering than it used to. A push toward the center, against the model's lean, a small or accidental one, one with no lane lines to measure it, or one that ends in another lane changes nothing.
- **Low speed:** below 10 m/s the fixed 10 m lookahead makes the correction fade with speed squared. The car is already centered there and town lane lines are the least trustworthy.
- **Lateral control inactive, or the lateral maneuver tool supplying the curvature:** nothing is added and the filter resets, so the correction always ramps back in from zero.
- **A modelV2 message that is not shaped like one** (missing lines, x and y of different lengths, a plan with non-finite distances, a non-finite result): no correction. Stock reads a single number from the model here, so a bad message cannot crash `controlsd`; this reads arrays, and returns zero for the malformed shapes in the tests rather than raising.

The idea of correcting the plan toward the lane lines comes from StarPilot's lane centering, the heading term and the one-line hold from gm1500's `lp-e2e-blend`; the control law, gates and numbers here are this branch's own, from the measurements below.

No new telemetry fields are added. While engaged on the ordinary model-steering path, the correction is `controlsState.desiredCurvature` minus `modelV2.action.desiredCurvature` wherever the limits are not active. Exact recomputation of the correction and learned width needs `modelV2`, `carState`, and the enable/disable history; note the toggle times for A/B log review. `lagd` keeps learning the steering delay from `controlsState.desiredCurvature`, which is the command the car actually receives, correction included; `torqued` does not read it.

### Why these numbers

Measured on the Palisade: four drives of 2026-09-10 to 09-14 (68 min engaged and hands-off) for the first design, then the two drives of 2026-09-19 (52 min, 25 min measurable) on the current upstream model, which sits further left.

| | |
|---|---|
| Car's offset from the lane center | mean 0.21 m left, rms 0.34 m; 39 % of the time more than 0.3 m off; the body within 15 cm of a line 17 % of the time |
| Model's own recentring | the plan keeps 94 % of an offset after 1 s: it returns to its own spot over about 17 s |
| In curves | 0.39 m inside in left curves, 0.19 m inside in firm right curves |
| Lines | both usable 72 % of engaged time, exactly one 21 % (left only 13 %, right only 7 %), neither 8 %; one-line stretches are mostly under a second but the long ones hold most of the time: a 1 s hold covers 44 % of it, 20 s covers 97 % |
| Lane width | median 3.29 m, p1–p99 2.67–4.49 m; it drifts 0.12 m over 1 s and 0.24 m over 30 s at the 90th percentile |
| Steering latency, desired to actual curvature | 0.30–0.35 s (cross-correlation), 0.29 s (`lateralDelay`) |

The correction adds position stiffness, so latency between the command and the car turns into negative damping that the model's own heading control has to absorb. The heading term supplies the damping the lookahead alone lacks. In a closed-loop replay of the two drives, with the recorded lane lines and a driving model fitted to the numbers above, the plain lookahead law weaves with an 8–10 s period once the model straightens up slowly (2 s) or the latency reaches 0.45 s; with the heading term at twice the lookahead it does not weave in any case tried, including 3 s and 0.45 s, and the car moves sideways 15 % less than it did in the recording instead of 2 % more. A heading gain of 1.5 still weaves in the worst case; leaving the error unbounded reaches 0.4 m/s²; a longer lookahead removes the weave but centres worse, since authority falls with its square. The rest of the replay: rms offset 0.34 → 0.13 m, time more than 0.3 m off 39 % → 4 %, body within 15 cm of a line 17 % → 1 %, left curves 0.39 → 0.05 m; active 78 % of engaged time (62 % without the hold); correction p99 0.18 and max 0.202 m/s²; jerk p99 0.29 and max 0.68 m/s³.

The one-line hold was checked on 1,482 recorded stretches where the lost line came back: the center rebuilt from the other line and the learned width was within 0.16 m of the true one 90 % of the time, and the error does not grow with how long the hold lasts — it is set by why the line was lost. Where it was lost at an exit or a merge the width is wrong, which is what the plan check is for: it drops 87 % of the holds that would have ended more than 0.3 m off and 3 % of the good ones. The snap rule comes from a right turn off a 4.2 m road onto a 3.3 m one with only the left line: the 5 s filter kept the wide width for two minutes and the check had to drop nearly every hold, while a width taken from a 0.4 s glimpse of both lines makes the rest of that road centred. Fleet-wide the snap fires about once a minute and makes the hold error smaller, not larger.

The fade change and the driver push were checked on 26 drives of 2026-09-22 to 09-27 that ran the previous version (5.7 h engaged above 10 m/s). Replaying the recorded messages through the previous code reproduces the correction the car logged (correlation 0.94, median difference 0.0004 m/s²). With the same inputs, the fade change differs from it by more than 0.02 m/s² 1 % of the time, and 5.8 % of the time in curves above 1 m/s², where the mean correction is 4.4 % smaller. The steering wheel was pressed 1,055 times while engaged (186 per hour, mostly hands resting on it); 4 of those presses would have been taken as agreement with the model (under one an hour), all of them maneuvers at turns or lane changes that ended within 4 s. In the same logs the plan moves left after positive driver torque (correlation −0.54 between the torque and the plan's movement to the right over the next 0.5 s), which is the sign the push check uses. In the closed-loop test, a model holding 0.4 m left past cones is centred to within 0.1 m; a one-second push left leaves the car at 0.4 m for as long as the model wants it there, and it is centred again once the model's preferred spot returns to the middle.

### What it costs

- A deliberate move of less than 0.9 m gets smaller, unless the driver pushes the car over with it. Against a model holding a 0.6 m offset the estimate is about 0.5 m; beyond 0.9 m nothing changes.
- After a push the way the model leans, the car is not centred until the model comes back within 0.15 m of the center by itself or two minutes pass. The model rarely centres itself, so the two minutes is usually what ends it; push back toward the center to end it sooner.
- The lane center is measured from the camera. A device mounted off the car's centerline moves "center" by the same amount; the left offset grows with speed, which a mount error would not do, so most of it is how the model drives — but check the mount if the car ends up right of center.
- With one line and no width learned in the last 60 s, or with neither line (8 % of engaged time), it does nothing.
- Real lanes narrower than 2.5 m exist (a 2.4 m stretch in the logs); the width gate stands the two-line correction down there.

## Calm steering

The owner's other complaint, with lane centering on and off alike: small steering corrections that jump too quickly. That is the stock torque controller, not the centering. Besides the model's lateral acceleration and the P and I terms, `LatControlTorque` adds a friction term, a ramp that puts up to ±0.33 m/s² (0.13 of full torque, about 50 counts) behind any error over 0.2 m/s². Its slope is about twice the P gain at highway speed, and it reads the measured steering angle, whose 0.1° sensor steps are worth more lateral acceleration the faster the car goes: at 30 m/s one step moves the torque by 0.016 (6.5 counts at 409) in a single frame, 0.011 of it from the friction ramp. It also reads each 20 Hz step of the model's plan, through the jerk look-ahead, as a short pulse. Nothing between the controller and the car smooths either.

**On a highway straight the friction term goes through a 0.1 s low-pass filter.** Only that term: the model's lateral acceleration, roll, the learned offset, P and I are untouched. The filtered value is blended in by a gate made of smooth ramps, so nothing steps when it opens or closes; it closes at once and opens over a quarter of a second. With the gate shut the controller is stock to the bit. The filter runs every frame, engaged or not, so it is never stale when the gate opens.

It is off, gate exactly 0:

- **Below 20 m/s** (72 km/h). It opens fully by 28 m/s (101 km/h), the owner's cruising speed.
- **Not straight:** once the desired or the measured steering angle reaches 3° (0.5 m/s² at 30 m/s); it starts closing from 0°.
- **Moving:** while the desired or the measured angle changes faster than 8°/s (1.3 m/s³ at 30 m/s), starting from 5°/s. The desired angle's rate is read over 0.1 s, the measured angle's over 0.2 s, so one 0.1° sensor step reads 0.5°/s and does not shut it.
- **Hands on the wheel** (steering pressed) or lateral control inactive.
- For the first 0.2 s after start, until both rate windows are full.

### Why these numbers

All but the speed edge are BLaTv3's highway feedforward taper, verbatim, which used the same gates and filter on the whole feedforward. On BLaTv3 route 4d at 120–150 km/h near straight, the frame-to-frame change of the feedforward correlated 0.93 with the measured angle's noise and 0.02 with the model's target, and the same angle noise made a 28 times larger feedforward swing at highway speed than in town. On route 54 (47 min, replayed on its own logged inputs) the taper cut the straight-road torque step above 100 km/h by 48 % at the median and reversals from 10.9 to 1.3 a minute, with turn-in, overshoot and unwind timing unchanged; the owner found the highway calmer and curves fine. Here only the friction term is filtered: in a simulation made while designing it, that costs 20–30 ms less on a move for about 4 points less of the reduction, and it improves the phase margin on every plant tried. The speed edge is this branch's own: full at 28 m/s instead of 30, opening from 20, because the owner cruises at 28–29 m/s. Replaying the owner's route 000001c3 (105 s of highway straight, median 28.8 m/s, a 2° road bank), the gate's mean rises from 0.57 to 0.67, the mean torque step from 0.0092 to 0.0086 against stock's 0.0133, and the remaining limiter is the ~1° the wheel holds against the bank. A gated 3-frame average of the measured lateral acceleration, for the P term's share of the sensor steps, was built and reviewed and is held back: on plants with this car's 0.33 s delay it can settle into a sustained 1.6 Hz cycle of about 11 counts that stock holds flat.

The rest is simulation, from this branch's tests on the Palisade at 30 m/s:

| | stock | calm |
|---|---|---|
| Sensor staircase (0.3° wander, 0.1° steps) | largest step 0.0157, 194 reversals/min | 0.0061, 46/min; no bias |
| Sensor flip-flopping between two steps | mean step 0.0042, 1,560 reversals/min | 0.0019, 513/min |
| Model plan dithering ±0.02 m/s² at 20 Hz | mean step 0.023 | 0.009 |
| Closed loop on a 0.1 m/s² hold, 0.15 s lag + 0.1 s delay, sensor steps only | limit cycle 0.032 peak-to-peak, 330 reversals/min | 0.0005, none |

With 0.12 s of lag instead, stock's cycle is 0.064 peak-to-peak (0.096 under the 409 limits) at about 350 reversals a minute and calm brings it to 0.024 and 288 a minute: reduced, not flattened. With 0.13 of stiction in the simulated rack the wheel sticks on the hold and neither controller cycles. On a plant that stock cannot hold steady (0.2 s lag, 0.2 s delay, 0.1 s assumed delay) the gate opens and shuts with the swing and the swing is 6–9 % larger than stock's; held fully open it would be 19–26 % smaller. The tests hold calm to within 10 % of stock there, and to within 0.005 of stock wherever stock holds steady. In the flip-flop case a third of stock's reversals are left: the P term alone moves 0.005 on each step.

### What it costs

- A gentle highway move at 1–2.5°/s builds torque 4 frames (40 ms) later than stock to 25 % and 4–5 frames later to 63 % (the filter's time constant is 10 frames). A turn-in at 8°/s takes the gate below half within 8 frames, shuts it by 15, and keeps stock's timing.
- Between 20 and 28 m/s it is only partly open: half at 24 m/s.
- `controlsState.lateralControlState.torqueState.f` is the whole feedforward (trajectory, roll, offset and friction), so while the gate is open its friction part is the blended one, and process-replay's `controlsd` reference outputs change on highway-straight frames.
- Upstream's other lever for the sensor steps, `steeringAngleDeadzoneDeg` in the car's torque tuning (Toyota uses 0.2°), is in opendbc and is not used here: in the design simulation with sensor steps only it raised hunting, by 20–94 % in mean step. If it is tried, A/B it on the car.
- Not field validated.

### Checking it

`tools/scripts/calm_steering_replay.py <route or rlog path> [--min-run-s 5]` replays a route's logged inputs through the controller twice, stock and calm, and prints per speed band (0–20, 20–60, 60–100, 100–150 km/h), over straight runs of at least 5 s (engaged, hands off, under 3°), the largest step, mean step and reversals a minute, the gate's duty, and the friction and lateral-acceleration factor in use. Its PARITY line compares the stock replay with the logged output; it is about 0 on a route driven on this controller and a warning is printed when it is not, as on BLaT routes. The replay is open loop on the logged steering angle, so it shows how much of that angle's noise reaches the torque, not whether a closed-loop limit cycle stops; the closed-loop tests are the check for that.
