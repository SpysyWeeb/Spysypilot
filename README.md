## What it does

**Lane changes trigger on the turn signal alone** — no steering nudge needed to confirm. With guardrails so it never fires when you didn't mean it:

- **One auto change per blinker activation.** A second change on the same blinker needs a manual nudge; cycling the blinker re-arms the automatic one.
- **Braking cancels the automatic change for the whole blinker event.** If you touch the brake with the blinker on, that blinker activation requires a manual nudge — the change will *not* fire the moment you lift off the pedal. Re-arm by cycling the blinker.

*(inspired by sunnypilot)*

**The car starts accelerating as soon as the change starts, when the lane it is moving into is clear** and the set speed is above the current speed. Today the car keeps braking behind the car it is pulling out to pass until the model moves its lead into the new lane, about 1.5 s into the change; the first positive acceleration request comes at 2.4 s and the car typically loses about 1 m/s across an overtaking lane change. With this, the planner's time gap to the car being left behind drops to 0.6 s the moment the change starts, so the cruise acceleration takes over and the MPC targets that relaxed distance (its danger-zone constraint does not bind at highway speed, so the module has to stand down itself). The target lane counts as clear when the blind spot monitor is quiet and the front radar sees no car in that lane that the planner would already be following after the change (inside the MPC's own follow distance for a car at that speed, judged where it will be 3 s ahead). The relaxation ends for the rest of the change as soon as the model hands the lead over to the new lane, when that lead brakes, when the driver backs out (blinker off, counter-steer, a gas or brake override) or after 2.5 s, and normal following resumes at once. The braking read comes from three cues: the radar's filtered deceleration beyond 1 m/s² (which lags a hard brake by about half a second), a raw lead speed drop of 0.5 m/s since the change started (before the filtered cue in all 1,149 hard brakes in the field data, by 1 s at the median), and the model's own lead acceleration estimate (earlier still in 41 % of them). Even so a hard brake is read 0.2–0.5 s after it starts; in a case where the stock gap is already at its limit, that delay and the shorter gap can cost the couple of metres stock has to spare. That is the price of relaxing the gap at all, and the reason the relaxation is short and stands down on every cue.

## How it works

Nudgeless: all in `DesireHelper`. After a short delay (`LANE_CHANGE_NUDGELESS_DELAY`, 0.05 s) in the pre-lane-change state, the change starts as if the driver had nudged — gated by `auto_allowed`, which requires: no auto change already used this blinker event (`nudgeless_used` latch) and no brake press seen this blinker event (`brake_cancelled` latch, set on `brakePressed` and only cleared when the blinker cycles off). Manual torque always works exactly as stock.

Acceleration: `LaneChangeGap` in the longitudinal planner owns the follow gap while `laneChangeState` is `laneChangeStarting`. It arms on the frame the change starts, remembering the lead at that moment (radar track id, with distance and speed continuity for an id change or a vision-only lead) and, while that lead is still `leadOne`, the target lane is clear and `vCruise - vEgo > 0.5 m/s`, returns a negative `t_follow_pad` that takes the MPC's time gap from the personality's value down to `LANE_CHANGE_T_FOLLOW` (0.6 s); the MPC applies it only while lead0 is the binding obstacle. The pad is recomputed every frame (a personality change keeps the 0.6 s) and snaps to zero the moment the relaxation ends: the lead handoff (a different car, or the lead disappearing), the lead braking (filtered deceleration beyond 1 m/s², raw speed 0.5 m/s below its best since the start, or the model's lead acceleration beyond 1 m/s²), the blinker going off, a counter-steer, a planner reset (gas or brake override) or 2.5 s after the start. The braking and driver cases also end the cruise acceleration for that change (`accelerate`, which combo's comfort ramp reads); the handoff and the time limit do not. The feature needs a front radar and a blind spot monitor (`CarParams.radarUnavailable` false, `enableBsm` true) and does nothing otherwise. The target lane band comes from the model's lane lines (fallback 3.5 m) in the radar's left-positive frame; a return blocks when its distance at arrival (3 s of closing) is inside `(vEgo² − vTrack²) / (2 · COMFORT_BRAKE) + t_follow · vEgo + STOP_DISTANCE`, the MPC's comfort distance behind a car at that speed; the lane lines are sampled at each return's distance so a bend keeps the band on the lane; returns closer than 2 m are ignored (the blind spot monitor's job), a stationary return counts only when it is already inside the plain follow gap (stationary clutter is common: 17 of the 36 changes below had one inside the stopping distance), and oncoming traffic counts when it arrives inside the gap within 3 s; a blocked verdict holds for 0.5 s so flickering returns do not pump the gap.

Field evidence behind the numbers (2,802 segments, 248 lane changes with openpilot longitudinal active): in the 36 changes with a slower lead and headroom, the lead handed over at 1.47 s median, the mean acceleration request over the first 2 s was −0.22 m/s² (worst decile −1.06), the first request above 0.3 m/s² came at 2.4 s, and the car lost 0.9 m/s median. A 0.6 s gap puts 32 of those 36 outside the relaxed follow distance at the start of the change; 1.45 s only 5. Replaying the module over the same frames with combo's gaps (the owner drives on the aggressive personality, 1.0 s there and 1.25 s on this branch): it relaxes from the first frame of the change in 29 of the 36, for 0.8 s median, never before the change starts, and leaves changes without headroom alone; the relaxation ends at the model's handoff in 17, on a car already in the target lane in 8, on a braking lead in 3, at the 2.5 s limit in 2 and on the driver backing out in 1. The 7 that never relax start with a car inside the follow distance in the target lane. The radar's lateral estimate matches the model's lead position (slope 0.94, median error 0.18 m), and adjacent-lane cars sit at ±3–4.5 m.

## What changed

- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/desire_helper.py` — nudgeless auto-start with delay, the one-per-blinker latch, and the brake latch-cancel.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/lane_change_gap.py` — new: the lane change gap owner and the target lane check.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/longitudinal_planner.py` — runs it and passes its pad to the MPC.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/longitudinal_mpc_lib/long_mpc.py` — `update()` takes a `t_follow_pad`, applied while lead0 binds.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/plannerd.py` — subscribes to `radarTracks` (optional, the plan's validity does not depend on it); `process_replay.py` feeds it too.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/test/longitudinal_maneuvers/plant.py` — the test plant carries `radarTracks` and SubMaster's liveness bookkeeping.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/tests/test_lane_changes.py`, `test_lane_change_gap.py` — tests.
