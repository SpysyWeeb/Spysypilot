## What it does

**Lane changes trigger on the turn signal alone** — no steering nudge needed to confirm. With guardrails so it never fires when you didn't mean it:

- **One auto change per blinker activation.** A second change on the same blinker needs a manual nudge; cycling the blinker re-arms the automatic one.
- **Braking cancels the automatic change for the whole blinker event.** If you touch the brake with the blinker on, that blinker activation requires a manual nudge — the change will *not* fire the moment you lift off the pedal. Re-arm by cycling the blinker.

*(inspired by sunnypilot)*

**The car starts accelerating as soon as the change starts, when the lane it is moving into is clear** and the set speed is above the current speed. Today the car keeps braking behind the car it is pulling out to pass until the model moves its lead into the new lane, about 1.5 s into the change; the first positive acceleration request comes at 2.4 s and the car typically loses about 1 m/s across an overtaking lane change. With this, the planner's time gap to the car being left behind drops to 0.6 s the moment the change starts, so the cruise acceleration takes over and the MPC targets that relaxed distance (its danger-zone constraint does not bind at highway speed, so the module has to stand down itself). The target lane counts as clear when the blind spot monitor is quiet and the front radar sees no car in that lane that the planner would already be following after the change (inside the MPC's own follow distance for a car at that speed, judged where it will be 3 s ahead). The relaxation ends for the rest of the change as soon as the model hands the lead over to the new lane, when that lead brakes, when the driver backs out (blinker off before the car is across, counter-steer, a gas or brake override), when the release's floor (below) hands a lead back, or after 2.5 s, and normal following resumes at once. The braking read comes from three cues: the radar's filtered deceleration beyond 1 m/s² (which lags a hard brake by about half a second), a raw lead speed drop of 0.5 m/s since the change started (before the filtered cue in all 1,149 hard brakes in the field data, by 1 s at the median), and the model's own lead acceleration estimate (earlier still in 41 % of them). Even so a hard brake is read 0.2–0.5 s after it starts; in a case where the stock gap is already at its limit, that delay and the shorter gap can cost the couple of meters stock has to spare. That is the price of relaxing the gap at all, and the reason the relaxation is short and stands down on every cue.

**The car stops braking for a car it is passing.** A slower car cannot be passed at any gap, and about 1.5 s into the change the model's lead slides off it onto the lane being left. On the drive that prompted this the planner stayed bound to leads 1 to 5 m off the planned path for 2.3 s, the speed bottomed at 24.8 mph, and the cruise acceleration only took over when the model dropped the last of them. While the change is starting, the target lane is clear and you have not backed out, the planner now stops following a radar lead the planned path has moved 1 m away from toward the old lane. Once the car being passed has gone off the path, it also stops following a lead only the camera sees, when that lead is itself 1 m off the path toward the old lane, the model cannot place it and radar sees nothing on the path; a camera-only lead on the path is always followed, since the model's range on such a lead can run 5 to 15 m long. A stopped lead is always followed, and so is one the planner could not take back gently, until the car being passed is beside you. The dash still shows the lead. FCW only warns for leads the planner follows, so it does not warn for a released one, and this car has no AEB under openpilot longitudinal.

**The target lane is read correctly while the lines go uncertain.** Mid crossing the model's line probabilities drop to zero and the check used to fall back to a fixed lane that reached into the next lane over: on the same drive a pickup two lanes over held off the acceleration for 1.5 s. The check now follows the line being crossed by where the model still draws it, across the model relabelling it, and stops checking the target lane once the car is across. It no longer takes a stationary return on the target lane's outer edge for a stopped car.

*(personal idea)*

## How it works

Nudgeless: all in `DesireHelper`. After a short delay (`LANE_CHANGE_NUDGELESS_DELAY`, 0.05 s) in the pre-lane-change state, the change starts as if the driver had nudged — gated by `auto_allowed`, which requires: no auto change already used this blinker event (`nudgeless_used` latch) and no brake press seen this blinker event (`brake_cancelled` latch, set on `brakePressed` and only cleared when the blinker cycles off). Manual torque always works exactly as stock.

Acceleration: `LaneChangeGap` in the longitudinal planner owns the follow gap while `laneChangeState` is `laneChangeStarting`. It arms on the frame the change starts, remembering the lead at that moment (radar track id, with distance and speed continuity for an id change or a vision-only lead) and, while that lead is still `leadOne`, the target lane is clear and `vCruise - vEgo > 0.5 m/s`, returns a negative `t_follow_pad` that takes the MPC's time gap from the personality's value down to `LANE_CHANGE_T_FOLLOW` (0.6 s); the MPC applies it only while lead0 is the binding obstacle. The pad is recomputed every frame (a personality change keeps the 0.6 s) and snaps to zero the moment the relaxation ends: the lead handoff (a different car, or the lead disappearing), the lead braking (filtered deceleration beyond 1 m/s², raw speed 0.5 m/s below its best since the start, or the model's lead acceleration beyond 1 m/s²), the blinker going off before the car is across, a counter-steer, a planner reset (gas or brake override), the floor below handing a lead back, or 2.5 s after the start. The braking and driver cases also end the cruise acceleration for that change (`accelerate`, which combo's comfort ramp reads); the handoff and the time limit do not. The feature needs a front radar and a blind spot monitor (`CarParams.radarUnavailable` false and the brand's `HAS_BSM` flag, which replaced `enableBsm` in opendbc) and does nothing otherwise. The target lane band comes from the model's lane lines (fallback 3.5 m) in the radar's left-positive frame, or from the crossed line below; a return blocks when its distance at arrival (3 s of closing) is inside `(vEgo² − vTrack²) / (2 · COMFORT_BRAKE) + t_follow · vEgo + STOP_DISTANCE`, the MPC's comfort distance behind a car at that speed; the lane lines are sampled at each return's distance so a bend keeps the band on the lane; returns closer than 2 m are ignored (the blind spot monitor's job), a stationary return counts only when it is already inside the plain follow gap (stationary clutter is common: 17 of the 36 changes below had one inside the stopping distance), and oncoming traffic counts when it arrives inside the gap within 3 s; a stationary return in the band's outer 0.9 m (`ROADSIDE_MARGIN`) is the roadside, where a stopped car in the lane cannot sit, while oncoming traffic there still counts, since that is where it drives on a two-lane road; a blocked verdict holds for 0.5 s so flickering returns do not pump the gap.

Lead release: `LaneChangeGap.update()` decides every frame which of radard's two leads the MPC follows, and the planner hands the MPC `lane_change_gap.followed(radarState)`: radard's message with the released leads marked not present, or the very same object when nothing is released. radarState, `hasLead` and everything else see radard's leads as published. The release needs `laneChangeStarting`, a clear target lane and no back-out; the set-speed headroom gates only the relaxation and `accelerate`, so reaching the set speed mid pass does not hand back the car being passed. A radar lead is released when its offset from the planned path at its own distance, toward the lane being left, is over 1.0 m (`LEAD_OFF_PATH`), and the same car (its radar id, or 2 m / 1.5 m/s continuity) stays released above 0.5 m (`LEAD_ON_PATH`); the path at the lead's distance moves with a bend and the car's yaw like a car in the lane does. A camera-only lead needs the same offsets from the path, the car being passed (followed by its radar track id for the whole change) more than 1.0 m off the path, the model's lateral std on it at least 1.0 m (0.8 m to stay released), no radar return within 1.0 m of the path (0.5 m to stay) from 2 m out to radard's own match window past it, and the car not yet across. leadTwo shares leadOne's verdict when it is the same car, unless it sits on the target side, and is judged on its own otherwise; a braking leadTwo is never released, and a released one that brakes backs the change out like leadOne. Far out the path can stray 1 m from a car still in its lane, but a far lead rarely binds and the floor bounds it. There is no timer: the release ends with the change, a back-out, a blocked target lane or the path coming back, and a lead handed back is in the MPC's solve on the frame it is handed back.

The floor (`too_close()`): a lead is judged as if handed back after the car keeps its current acceleration for 1 s (`RESTORE_LAG`). It is too close when stopping the closing before the follow distance (`t_follow · vLead + STOP_DISTANCE`, the personality's gap without the relaxation) needs more than 1 m/s² of steady braking (`RESTORE_DECEL`), or, already inside that distance, while it is still closing or within three quarters of it. That bounds the steady braking; the MPC itself, closing at 5 to 7 m/s on a lead handed back at that boundary, aims further back and peaks at about 1.1 to 1.7 times it. A stopped or crawling lead (under 2 m/s) is never released, and a lead too close is never newly released. Once a lead, or the car being passed after the model has moved its lead elsewhere, has been too close for 3 frames running, every lead that is not beside the car is followed at the full gap, and the relaxation ends, until the car being passed is 2.0 m beside the car (`LEAD_BESIDE`), measured from the crossed line so a bend does not put a car still in its lane beside it.

Crossed line: whenever the model places the car's own lines before the change (probability above 0.5, or std under 0.3 m while it doubts them), they are remembered. On the change's first frame the line on the change side, if placed within the last 1.0 s of driving outside a change, seeds the crossed line; through the change it follows the placed line nearest where it was, within 1.35 m, which carries it across the model's relabel, and with no placed line near it is held for 1.0 s before the band falls back to the default. The band runs from that line (kept 1.0 m off the car's path) to one lane width past it (the last confident width) and bends with the line. Once the car's center is 0.5 m past the line, the radar band check stops; the blind spot monitor and radar health still block. After 3 frames running in which the model places the line 0.5 m past the car, the car is across: switching the blinker off then finishes the change instead of backing out, and no camera-only lead is released.

Field evidence behind the numbers (2,802 segments, 248 lane changes with openpilot longitudinal active): in the 36 changes with a slower lead and headroom, the lead handed over at 1.47 s median, the mean acceleration request over the first 2 s was −0.22 m/s² (worst decile −1.06), the first request above 0.3 m/s² came at 2.4 s, and the car lost 0.9 m/s median. A 0.6 s gap puts 32 of those 36 outside the relaxed follow distance at the start of the change; 1.45 s only 5. Replaying the module over the same frames with combo's gaps (the owner drives on the aggressive personality, 1.0 s there and 1.25 s on this branch): it relaxes from the first frame of the change in 29 of the 36, for 0.8 s median, never before the change starts, and leaves changes without headroom alone; the relaxation ends at the model's handoff in 17, on a car already in the target lane in 8, on a braking lead in 3, at the 2.5 s limit in 2 and on the driver backing out in 1. The 7 that never relax start with a car inside the follow distance in the target lane. The radar's lateral estimate matches the model's lead position (slope 0.94, median error 0.18 m), and adjacent-lane cars sit at ±3–4.5 m.

Replaying the module over 900 logged lane changes (2,703 segments) at combo's gaps: the first return to block a change sits more than 4.5 m to the side 66 times, against 295 with the fixed-lane fallback (stationary ones 18 against 213); a lead is released in 243 changes, 116 of them with openpilot longitudinal active; no release happens on the target side. Camera frames of the changes with a camera-only release under 40 m showed the model over-ranging such leads and, twice, a lead on the target side released; the release rules above come from that check.

## What changed

- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/desire_helper.py` — nudgeless auto-start with delay, the one-per-blinker latch, and the brake latch-cancel.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/lane_change_gap.py` — new: the lane change gap owner, the lead release and its floor, the target lane check with the crossed line and the roadside margin, and the enable check on the brands' blind spot flags.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/longitudinal_planner.py` — runs it, passes its pad to the MPC and plans on `lane_change_gap.followed(radarState)`.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/longitudinal_mpc_lib/long_mpc.py` — `update()` takes a `t_follow_pad`, applied while lead0 binds.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/plannerd.py` — subscribes to `radarTracks` (optional, the plan's validity does not depend on it); `process_replay.py` feeds it too.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/test/longitudinal_maneuvers/plant.py` — the test plant carries `radarTracks` and SubMaster's liveness bookkeeping.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/tests/test_lane_changes.py`, `test_lane_change_gap.py` — tests.
- `openpilot/selfdrive/controls/tests/test_lane_change_release.py` — new: the release through the real planner and MPC, every restore cue, and closed-loop lane changes.
